Where wealth is held

A rounded reference picture of global household wealth — about $470T at the end of 2024, after the UBS Global Wealth Report 2025. Shares are whole percentages of the part above them; the dollars are derived from the total. Seven levels deep — six of detail, then where in the world it sits. The decade charts (2015–2024) read the reports' 56-market sample: the world's line is the report's own record, the four regions are anchored on its 2023 and 2024 tables and walked back at their stated since-2008 growth, and everything deeper is assumed — so the charts are the sample's history, not every household. Growth rates are nominal USD assumptions built on the published long-run record, not forecasts; the projections are that arithmetic, nothing more. Static, not live data, and not accounting.

Research

Give it a goal — a sentence or two about what you want to understand or decide. It reads the open textbooks and papers on that goal, then writes one saved report, and its final part ties the findings to your own data.

Settings

Your own language-model key. Everything in this app that uses a model — the ask, the fills, the AI backtest, the notes — runs on it. The key is stored only in this browser and sent with each request; the app never stores or logs it. If the server is deployed with its own key, yours simply takes precedence when set.

Docs

One repository, one Python process, no database, no cloud. This is how to run it, how the key works, and the rules that keep the numbers honest.

Run it

Python 3.10 or newer. That is the whole dependency story — a FastAPI app and its usual small stack.

git clone https://github.com/akirinsolutions/longview.git && cd longview
python -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/uvicorn app.main:app --port 8010

Open http://127.0.0.1:8010. On first write the app creates data/longview.json — one file holding every section: your portfolio, your notes, your risk moves, your followed funds, your research reports, the economy tree. It is private to your machine and untracked in the repository.

The model key

Every feature that uses a language model — the ask, the risk fills, the AI backtest, research — resolves its model from the same place, in this order:

  1. Your key, per request. Set it in Settings (base URL, key, model). It is stored in this browser's local storage and sent with each request as an X-Longview-LLM header.
  2. The server's key. If you have not set one, the app uses LONGVIEW_LLM_KEY, LONGVIEW_LLM_BASE, and LONGVIEW_LLM_MODEL from its environment (the old PROOF_LLM_* names are still read as a fallback).
  3. No key. Everything else in the app still works — only the AI features go quiet, and they say so plainly.

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint works: OpenAI, a hosted provider, or your own server. The key is relayed to your endpoint and never stored or logged on the app.

Your data

There is no login and no account. Everything you keep in the app lives in one file on the app's machine: data/longview.json — portfolio, notes, risk moves, followed funds, research reports, the economy tree. Your browser holds a mirror of it; when you open the app the two compare and the newer one wins.

In Settings you can download the file — that is your whole data, portable to any Longview — and import one back. Nothing you enter leaves except the market-data lookups (public sources: Yahoo Finance, SEC EDGAR — they see nothing of yours) and, when a model key is set, the requests to your own model endpoint: your question or goal, a digest of your data, and the key.

The math, honestly

No lookahead: a backtest evaluates each day only with prices and information known on that day, and a strategy's research reads only the pages that existed when it runs. No survivorship: the data is what was traded, not what is still around. When the model's answer would move money the plan never approved, the move is dropped and the log says why. A result you cannot audit is not a result.
